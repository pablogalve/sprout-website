Best apps to block short videos in 2026

Updated 4 August 2026

The best app for blocking short videos depends on whether you want the feed gone, delayed or budgeted. Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing are free and block by app. one sec delays the opening. Opal blocks on a schedule. Sprout blocks against a daily limit you earn back. Here is what each is genuinely good at, and where each falls down.

1. Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing, free

Good at: costs nothing, already installed, blocks any app, and on iOS the passcode makes it hard to undo if somebody else sets it.

Falls down: the block screen offers one more minute and ignore for today. If you set your own passcode, you can also remove it.

Start here. If it holds, you do not need anything else on this list.

2. one sec, friction

Good at: a breathing pause before the app opens, which kills reflexive launches. Cheap, unobtrusive, and effective for the opens you did not consciously choose.

Falls down: it never says no. A deliberate scroll gets through every time.

3. Opal, scheduled blocking

Good at: the strongest scheduled blocking in this list, with genuinely good usage reports. If you want apps unavailable during working hours, this is the direct answer.

Falls down: priced at the top of the category, and schedules only help if your distraction happens on a schedule.

4. Forest, focus sessions

Good at: a pleasant, low-pressure timer for one session of work or study, and shared sessions that work well for groups.

Falls down: it does not block anything. Leaving is one swipe.

5. Sprout, a daily limit you earn back

Good at: the one thing none of the others do. You set a daily limit for the apps that pull you in, and when it runs out they are blocked. Minutes come back by completing an activity: pushups, reading, a focus session, a chore. There is no button that grants more time, the block lifts because you did something. Free to start, on iPhone and Android.

Falls down: if you want an unbreakable block during fixed hours and nothing else, a pure scheduler is a closer fit.

6. Turning the feature off inside the app

Good at: free and immediate. YouTube lets you hide the Shorts shelf for 30 days, TikTok has its own daily limit, Instagram lets you thin out suggested content.

Falls down: partial coverage, and every switch is controlled by the app you are trying to use less.

How to choose in one minute

  • Never tried anything: Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing, free, today.
  • You open apps without deciding to: one sec.
  • You need apps gone from nine to five: Opal.
  • You want a nicer study timer: Forest.
  • You keep granting yourself more time: Sprout, because more time has to be earned.

Try the earn-your-time approach

Sprout blocks the apps you choose once your daily limit runs out, and gives minutes back only when you complete an activity: exercise, reading, a focus session, a chore. There is no button that grants more time. Free to start, on iPhone and Android.
